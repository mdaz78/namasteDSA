Resources

Curated, high-trust sources. The lessons draw their facts from here — not from guesses.

Knowledge

Big-O Cheat Sheet (Eric Rowell) — bigocheatsheet.com

The canonical one-page visual: growth-rate chart plus best/average/worst tables for every common data structure and sorting algorithm.

Use for: quick lookup, the "which is faster" ranking, sorting-algorithm complexities.

Interview Cake — "Big O Notation: Time & Space Complexity"

Interview-focused explainer. Strong on how to talk about complexity, drop constants, worst vs. best case, and space complexity.

Use for: framing your analysis the way an interviewer wants to hear it. This is the primary source for Lesson 1.

MIT OpenCourseWare 6.006 — Introduction to Algorithms

Primary academic source. Lectures 1–2 cover asymptotic notation and models of computation rigorously.

Use for: when an intuition needs a solid foundation.

Book: A Common-Sense Guide to Data Structures and Algorithms — Jay Wengrow (2nd ed.)

The clearest beginner-to-interview bridge on Big-O. Chapters 1–4 are exactly this mission.

Use for: worked examples, building intuition before formalism.

Book: Introduction to Algorithms (CLRS) — Cormen, Leiserson, Rivest, Stein — Chapter 3

The reference definition of Θ, O, and Ω.

Use for: precise definitions, and later recurrence work.

freeCodeCamp — "Big O Cheat Sheet: Time Complexity Chart"

Free, readable summary with JS-flavoured examples.

Use for: a second explanation when one phrasing doesn't land.

NamasteDSA course (Akshay Saini) — Time & Space Complexity section

The course being followed.

Use for: staying aligned with the course's vocabulary and problem set.

Wisdom — communities

r/leetcode

Active, interview-focused.

Use for: "is my complexity analysis right?", sanity-checking approaches, seeing how others explain trade-offs in real interviews.

LeetCode Discuss — per-problem solution threads

Every popular problem has threads debating the Big-O of each approach.

Use for: comparing your analysis of a specific problem against a crowd.

Namaste Dev / Akshay Saini community

The course community.

Use for: questions tied to the course's exact framing.
